<ins> Abstract: </ins>  <br />
Over the last ten years, neutrino multi-messenger astronomy established itself as a completely
new field of research. The first association of IceCube neutrino events with an extragalactic
source in 2017 triggered an unprecedented interest in the search of astrophysical neutrino
counterparts. One potential candidate for sources of neutrinos is a class of luminous galactic
cores with an astrophysical jet pointing at Earth, called blazars. Previous studies on the
relationship between blazars and neutrinos mainly focus on single neutrino events and a special
selections of blazars. In this thesis I investigate possible correlations between seven years of
time-integrated neutrino data and one of the most extensive catalogues of known blazars using
a spatial cross-matching method following the work of Buson et al. 2022a. Thereby I find a
total of 11 associations between blazars and neutrino emission with a corresponding chance
probability of 2.9e-6 (4.7σ). This work also serves the purpose of critically discussing the
established method, exploring its limitations and discovering possible improvements.
